More and more homeowners in Arizona are seeing new possibilities within their own property.

Recent changes to state regulations have expanded the options for building ADUs on many residential properties. But before imagining what that new space will look like, there is something important to review:

**what your property actually allows.**

At Sunny Casita, we believe a good project starts with understanding the lot, the applicable rules, and the household’s needs from the beginning.

## Arizona opened up new possibilities for ADUs

State legislation requires municipalities with more than 75,000 residents to allow ADUs on lots where a single-family home is also permitted.

This has created new opportunities for homeowners looking to add an independent space to their property.

But having that option does not mean every lot works the same way.

## Having space does not always mean you can build there

A spacious backyard may seem like the perfect place for an ADU.

However, before designing, you need to review factors such as:

* the location of the main house;
* permitted lot coverage;
* access;
* existing utilities;
* easements;
* construction requirements;
* property-specific conditions.

In other words, it is not just about finding where an ADU fits, but understanding where it can actually work.

## Size matters, too

State regulations establish certain size limits for ADUs, although conditions may vary depending on the city and the characteristics of each property.

That is why, before deciding on a layout, bedrooms, or finishes, it is worth checking how much space can actually be allocated to the project.

## What about Phoenix?

Phoenix updated its rules to align with the state changes, and there are now more opportunities to add ADUs to certain residential properties.

The city provides for situations in which a property may include more than one ADU, depending on the lot’s characteristics and the applicable conditions.

This makes reviewing each property individually even more important before moving forward.

## Do not overlook easements and utilities

Some elements are not immediately visible, but they can completely change a project.

For example, a utility easement may limit where a structure can be placed.

State legislation includes specific restrictions on building over these areas.

That is why reviewing the lot from the start can help avoid significant changes later.

## If you have an HOA, review its rules as well

Living in a community with an HOA can add another layer of requirements.

Even when state and municipal regulations allow an ADU, you may also need to review the community’s private rules before making decisions about the project. The City of Phoenix recommends checking this when applicable.

## Before the design comes a key question

When we think about an ADU, it is easy to start imagining what it will look like.

The exterior.

The layout.

The finishes.

The interior space.

But before all of that, the question should be:

**What possibilities does my property actually offer?**

Understanding the lot’s conditions first can help you plan a much more realistic project from the start.

## Conclusion

The changes in Arizona have opened up new opportunities to build ADUs, but each property has different conditions.

Before choosing a design or deciding how much space you want to build, it is worth understanding what your lot allows and which requirements apply in your area.

**A good ADU does not start with a floor plan. It starts with knowing your property’s possibilities.**
